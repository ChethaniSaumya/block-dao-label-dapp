A Certificate-Led Identity

Where block-label.com should change so the website and the Dealer Rights Certificate read as one brand — reviewed against the current live site, ranked by impact.

Design proposal · prepared for review · August 2026
01 — The gap

Two different brands today

Website now

White surfaces, blue accent, geometric sans, flat cards. Reads as a clean B2B technology company — credible, but interchangeable with a hundred SaaS sites.

Certificate — and where the site should go

Deep navy ground, gold foil, engraved serif display, hairline rules, seals and corner ornament. Reads as issued, authenticated, and collectible — a credential, not a product page.

The certificate's authority comes from four devices, and every one of them can be carried into a website without literal copying: a dark ground that makes gold read as metal, an engraved serif for display type against a plain sans for reading, hairline rules and corner ornament instead of drop shadows, and seals and verification marks used as real UI, not decoration.

02 — What to change, in priority order

Section by section

Ranked by how much identity each one carries per hour spent.

1

The design system itself

Rebuild

Colour tokens, type pairing, button styles, surfaces, focus states. Nothing else should be touched first — every page inherits from here, and doing it once prevents a patchwork of half-restyled screens.

Do: invert to the navy ground; introduce gold as the single accent; pair an engraved serif for headings with the existing sans for body; replace shadow-based elevation with hairline borders and a low gold glow.

2

Homepage hero

/Rebuild

The certificate already contains the perfect hero image: a glowing globe wrapped in a network, seen from space. The site's hero is currently text and four competing buttons on white.

Do: dark cosmic ground echoing the certificate art; gold serif headline; cut the four calls-to-action down to two primary (dealer partnership, BDL token) with the rest demoted.

3

NFT certificate & authentication

This is where the identity and the product are the same thing. The certificate should be shown at full size, framed, with its serial system, the four issuance rounds and the holder's benefits presented as an official record.

Do: make the artwork the hero rather than a thumbnail; render the serial in the certificate's own typographic style; treat the verification result as a stamped seal, not a green tick.

4

Dealer programme

/dealers/dealer-inquiryRebuild

The certificate is literally titled Dealer Rights Certificate and names the holder an authorised dealer. These pages are the conversion path to owning one, so they carry more of the promise than any other page on the site.

Do: lead with the certificate itself as the thing being earned; present tiers and margins as an engraved schedule; give the application form the weight of an application, not a contact box.

Dealer dashboard

/dashboard/sales/referrals/rankingRestyle

Where dealers spend their actual time. A members' area is where "premium" either holds up or collapses — most brands stop at the marketing pages and leave the logged-in experience generic.

Do: dark data surfaces, gold only for emphasis and positive movement, tabular figures, ranking presented as standings rather than a plain table.

03 — Reorganisation, not just restyling

Two structural notes

The navigation is carrying nine top-level items

Company, Business, Technology, Content, NFT Certificate, Contact, Airdrop, Sign in, Sign up. Three of those — certificate, airdrop and token — are one story: the ecosystem a dealer joins. Grouping them under a single Ecosystem heading shortens the bar and makes the certificate the destination rather than one option among many.

Authenticity should be a running motif, not one badge

The certificate carries "Authenticity Verified" and a BSC mark. The site verifies certificates but does not otherwise show that it is blockchain-backed. Used consistently — on dealer profiles, on the certificate page, beside token figures — that verification mark becomes the visual signature that ties every page back to the certificate.

05 — Before starting

Questions worth settling

i

The certificate and the smart contract disagree on the name. The artwork reads "Dealer Rights Certificate — Authorized Dealer", while the deployed collection is named "Block Label Creator DAO Certificate" with the symbol BLDAO. Wallets and marketplaces show the contract name, so buyers would see a different product from the one on their certificate. Which is correct? It is a single transaction to change, with no redeployment.

ii

Certificate features already exist in two places. The website has NFT certificate authentication, NFT wholesale and dealer settlement in its admin area; the Web3 app has its own certificate and issuance pages. Deciding which owns what avoids building the same thing twice and paying for it twice.

iii

Four languages. Korean, English, Japanese and Chinese all appear in the site. A serif display face has to carry all four — the type choice needs testing in Korean and Japanese specifically, since an English-only pairing will fall apart there.

iv

How far does "dark" go? Full dark identity, or dark for hero and ecosystem pages with lighter surfaces for long-form reading? Worth deciding at the system stage rather than page by page.
